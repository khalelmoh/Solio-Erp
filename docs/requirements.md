# Funded Projects ERP — requirements, revision 6

This is a new application and new database. Its purpose is stewardship of funded projects, not sales or profit. The first release is a local, single-organization, USD-only implementation at two decimal places. Demonstration records must be explicitly labelled.

## People and authorization

Authentication uses salted scrypt password hashes, expiring server-side sessions and HttpOnly cookies. Project membership, checked by the server on every read and write, assigns requester, manager, finance or viewer access. Administrators configure policies, accounts, users and memberships but may not approve their own documents. A project creator becomes its manager. Users can only assign managers who are already organization users. Finance can post only for projects to which it has finance membership. Employee/HR/payroll records have separate organization permissions, encrypted salary terms/calculations, redacted audits and employee-only self-service access.

## Lifecycle

1. A manager creates a unique project code with name, description, department, manager, dates and activities. General operations use approved organization funds and independent operating budgets without a dummy project; department/cost-centre allocations are optional. Existing overhead projects retain their history.
2. A manager submits a complete budget version with category, activity and eligible period lines. Another authorized manager/finance approver approves it. Amendments and transfers are new complete versions referencing the previous version; original versions remain intact. Pending proposals confer no spending authority.
3. Finance records donor/organization promises and actual receipts independently. Receipts debit a shared bank/cash/mobile-money account and credit funding control, identifying project and source.
4. A requester submits one or more spending allocations. Each identifies a project, category, activity and source. All participating projects require membership. Amount-based approval levels are configurable, distinct approvers are required, self-approval is forbidden, and edits replace allocations and invalidate every approval.
5. Final spending approval reserves budget atomically. A purchase order converts the approved reservation into a purchase commitment without reserving twice. Partial bills release only the amount actually billed. Cancellation releases only the unused reservation; posted bills survive.
6. Finance posts a supplier bill, charging the budget once and debiting expense or asset while crediting accounts payable. A partial payment debits payable and credits project-allocated cash. It never charges the budget again. Supplier invoice references are unique per supplier. Payment references are unique per cash account.
7. Advance requests record beneficiary, employee/supplier type, due date and allocations before approval under the spending policy. Final approval reserves budget once. Disbursement debits advances and credits cash; actual cost remains zero. Accepted settlements require supporting receipts on new advance requests, debit expense/asset and credit advances, charge budget and release the matching reservation. Unused returns debit cash, credit advances and release unused reservations automatically. Show issued, settled, returned, outstanding and overdue amounts. Legacy generic requests remain usable with explicit disbursement terms; legacy records without terms use a labelled project-end due-date default.
8. Administrators enter/import bank, cash and mobile-money statements and partially match movements to existing book cash lines. Matching does not post expense or cash. Enforce account/transaction identity, direction, amounts and cutoff, preventing reuse on both sides. Finalization requires all bank movements matched, zero difference and review reasons for outstanding book entries. Unmatching finalized statements requires audited reopening. Explicit review can offset an unbanked book entry against its linked opposite correction.
9. Credit notes reduce unpaid payable first; credited paid amounts become supplier-refund receivables. Reverse expense/asset and budget charges; restore a commitment only for explicit replacement goods. Refund receipts clear receivables and increase cash without changing cost again. Posted corrections require linked reversals and dependency checks. Locked-period originals require independent named-poster authorization, with corrections in an open period; ordinary updates cannot unlock a closed period.
10. Reports distinguish promises, gross receipts, returned funding, unreceived promises, cash, bills, supplier credits, advance assets, advance reservations, purchase commitments and remaining authority. Filter project, category, source and period; distinguish closing balances from movements; provide source-record drill-down, CSV exports and printable statements.
11. Operational completion stops new spending but permits existing obligations to be liquidated. Financial closure separately checks commitments, bills, supplier receivables, advances, cash matching, open shared statements, residual source cash and unreceived promises. Unresolved items block closure unless an independent administrator authorizes the named closer and documents each balance, treatment, owner and expiry against a current snapshot. An exception does not clear balances. Financially closed projects block financial activity; administrator reopening requires a reason in immutable audit/history.

## Financial invariants

- Remaining budget = current approved budget − net actual budget charges − outstanding reservations/commitments. Each category/activity and period must also pass its budget check.
- Funding approval is not a receipt. Funds available for payment = project/source cash allocation; commitments and bills do not change cash. This excludes unreceived promises and does not net liabilities a second time.
- Every posted monetary transaction has balanced double-entry lines. Reservations are a separate ledger, not accounts payable.
- An asset purchase consumes budget at invoice value but debits an asset account; subsequent depreciation must not consume that purchase budget again. Advances consume budget only on accepted settlement. Payroll estimates are forecasts, never actuals; only posted payroll allocations become actuals.
- Spending is checked against funding source project, category and eligible date restrictions, using the document's eligible date. A source promise is an information record, not an automatic spending ceiling; independently approved, versioned grant ceilings are a separate configurable spending authority.
- Above-budget authority requires a named finance approver, a project-specific maximum overrun, a reason and expiry. It does not override source restrictions or lack of cash. Demo does not enable exceptions by default.
- Preserve posted journals, charges, commitments and approvals. Corrections are linked reversing journals plus reversing budget/commitment movements. Dependency checks prevent reversing a paid bill or funding whose cash has been spent.
- Monetary input is a decimal string; USD has two decimal places, excess precision is rejected (no implicit floating-point rounding). Storage uses exact integer minor units, representing fixed-scale decimals. Enforce safe integer bounds.
- Atomic database transactions serialize budget/cash checks and writes. Durable idempotency keys bind to actor and canonical payload. Reused keys with different payloads fail. Independent retries also encounter unique invoice/payment references.
